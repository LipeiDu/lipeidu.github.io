## Electromagnetic probes and multimessenger perspectives

In my recent work, I have been interested in developing electromagnetic probes as a complementary window into the dynamical evolution of the quark-gluon plasma (QGP). Unlike hadronic observables, photons and dileptons escape the medium with minimal reinteraction and thus retain information from earlier stages of the collision. This naturally motivates a multimessenger perspective, where different electromagnetic channels are combined to access both thermodynamic and dynamical properties of the system.

In these studies, I focused on how correlated electromagnetic emissions can be used to disentangle temperature and collective motion. The earlier analysis established the conceptual basis by showing that photon blue shift and dilepton-based temperature measures respond differently to radial flow. Building on that, the later work developed a more complete observable-oriented framework that connects electromagnetic radiation directly to early-time radial expansion of the QGP.

Taken together, these studies trace a progression from identifying the underlying sensitivity of electromagnetic probes to radial flow, to formulating a measurable framework for extracting that information from data. More broadly, they illustrate how correlated electromagnetic signals can probe the space-time evolution of strongly interacting matter and support a more integrated multimessenger description of heavy-ion collisions.

### Selected studies

#### Multimessenger study of baryon-charged QCD matter in heavy-ion collisions

Lipei Du  
*Phys. Lett. B 861* (2025), 139270

This work explored how correlated electromagnetic emissions can be used to disentangle temperature and collective motion in baryon-charged QCD matter. It showed that the blue shift of photon spectra and the effective temperature extracted from dilepton invariant mass distributions respond differently to radial flow, establishing the theoretical basis for separating flow effects from thermal scales using electromagnetic radiation alone. The analysis was formulated at the level of underlying emission properties rather than directly in experimentally accessible observables.

- [e-Print: 2408.08501 [nucl-th]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08501)
- [DOI: 10.1016/j.physletb.2025.139270](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physletb.2025.139270)

#### Electromagnetic Tomography of Radial Flow in the Quark-Gluon Plasma

Lipei Du, Ulrich Heinz  
*Phys. Rev. Lett. 136* (2026), 102301

This subsequent work developed the multimessenger idea into a more complete and observable-oriented framework. By identifying a robust relation between dilepton-based temperature measures and the blue shift of photon spectra, it constructed a practical observable directly sensitive to the early-time radial expansion of the QGP. The result extends the role of electromagnetic radiation beyond temperature characterization toward a quantitative probe of collective dynamics.

- [e-Print: 2505.20752 [hep-ph]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20752)
- [DOI: 10.1103/ptyq-cs9m](https://doi.org/10.1103/ptyq-cs9m)
